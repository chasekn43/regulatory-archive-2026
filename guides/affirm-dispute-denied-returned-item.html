The Anatomy of an Automated Return Dispute Rejection

In modern point-of-sale financing, a common failure mode occurs when a customer returns merchandise in full compliance with merchant policies, but the lender's automated dispute algorithm denies the claim. This typically happens because the lender relies on automated merchant API responses rather than verifying physical proof of return delivery.

Step-by-Step Forensic Escalation Protocol

Step 1: Download Certified Carrier Proof of Delivery (POD)

Do not simply take a screenshot of the delivery status. Download the carrier's official PDF Proof of Delivery document (from FedEx, UPS, or USPS) showing delivery timestamp, facility location, and receiving signature.

Step 2: Preserve Merchant RMA and Communication Headers

Export the full email thread showing the merchant issuing the return label or Return Merchandise Authorization (RMA). Ensure timestamp and sender header information is fully visible.

Step 3: Submit a Formal Written Notice of Dispute

Send a formal written letter to Affirm Customer Operations and Executive Relations asserting an unresolved billing error under 12 C.F.R. § 1026.13. Attach the Proof of Delivery and RMA records.

Step 4: Escalate to the CFPB and State Attorney General

If automated denials persist, file a formal complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) and your state Attorney General Consumer Protection Division, attaching your complete evidence package.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Affirm charge interest while a return dispute is being reviewed?
Under Regulation Z billing error rules, lenders may not accrue finance charges on disputed amounts if the error is resolved in the consumer's favor.
What if the merchant claims they never received the returned package?
If the return shipment was executed using a merchant-provided prepaid return shipping label, carrier delivery to the destination address transfers custody and liability to the merchant.
