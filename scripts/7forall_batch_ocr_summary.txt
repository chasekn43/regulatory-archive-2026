Thu, sep 25, 2025 at 1243 PM Under Federal Trade Commission (FTC) guidelines, a disclosure is considered "clear and conspicuous" if it is difficult to miss and easy for an ordinary consumer to understand . A company cannot bury or hide important terms like a "no cancellation policy" in fine print. The standard is a "performance standard," meaning its effectiveness is measured by whether an average consumer would actually notice and comprehend it. For a no-cancellation policy to be clear and conspicuous, it must be: • Prominent: The disclosure must be in a font size, color, and location that makes it easily noticeable. It should not be hidden at the very bottom of a long page or in an "About Us" section. • Proximate: The disclosure should appear close to the claim or action it qualifies. For an online purchase, this means placing the policy near the "add to cart" or "confirm purchase" buttons, or directly on the product page. • Presented clearly: The language must be simple, direct, and unambiguous. It should be easily understandable to the intended audience, with "legalese" or technical jargon avoided. • Unavoidable: In online transactions, the disclosure must be "unavoidable," meaning the consumer must be able to see it before completing the transaction.
