### PART II: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY OF SECURITIES VIOLATIONS

This Whistleblower submission provides primary-source, certified documentation establishing that **Affirm Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: AFRM)** has engaged in systemic securities violations, internal control breakdowns, and false regulatory filings:

1. **Falsification of Regulatory Filings (18 U.S.C. § 1001 & Exchange Act § 10(b))**:
   In formal written disclosures submitted to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB Complaint #260717-35668593), Affirm fabricated an external "$26.16 consumer bank chargeback" to disguise an internal ledger breakdown. Certified bank ACH clearing records establish that zero consumer chargebacks were ever initiated.

2. **Sarbanes-Oxley Internal Control Deficiencies (§§ 302 & 404)**:
   Affirm’s automated transaction clearing systems cannot reconcile internal single-use virtual card merchant adjustments with outstanding installment balances. When adjustments occur, Affirm’s software automatically locks performing consumer accounts and disables in-app payment rails to prevent internal ledger breaks from appearing on financial statements, manufacturing artificial customer defaults.

3. **Concealment of Non-Conforming Debt in Warehouse Facilities**:
   Affirm pledges consumer receivables to revolving credit facilities and Asset-Backed Securitization (ABS) structures (funded by Goldman Sachs, Barclays, and Morgan Stanley). Affirm’s credit agreements require pledged collateral to be free of active consumer disputes and legal claims. By algorithmically rejecting disputes in 16 minutes and misreporting balances, Affirm unlawfully preserves the borrowing base eligibility of non-conforming debt.

4. **Under-Provisioning of Credit Loss Reserves (CECL)**:
   By misclassifying disputed and uncollectible receivables as external chargebacks or active performing loans, Affirm artificially depresses its Allowance for Credit Losses under Current Expected Credit Losses (CECL) accounting standards, misleading public market investors.

### PART IV: STATUTORY CAUSES OF ACTION & SECURITIES VIOLATIONS

#### COUNT I: Material Misstatements in Regulatory Filings (18 U.S.C. § 1001 & Exchange Act § 10(b))
Affirm knowingly submitted false factual statements to a federal regulatory agency (CFPB) to disguise an internal accounting error. Mischaracterizing internal clearing discrepancies as consumer-initiated bank chargebacks constitutes an intentional deception to avoid regulatory penalties and audit scrutiny.

#### COUNT II: Ineffective Internal Accounting Controls (SOX §§ 302 & 404, 15 U.S.C. § 7241/7262)
Affirm's CEO and CFO certify the effectiveness of internal controls in quarterly SEC Form 10-Q filings. The documented inability of Affirm’s ledger to credit merchant adjustments without locking performing consumer lines represents a material weakness in internal controls over transaction processing.

#### COUNT III: Misrepresentations in ABS & Warehouse Borrowing Base Disclosures (Form ABS-EE)
Under Regulation AB, issuers must disclose repurchase demands and covenant breaches on securitized loans. By using automated bots to suppress dispute logging, Affirm avoids triggering mandatory loan repurchase clauses in its credit facilities with Goldman Sachs, Barclays, and Morgan Stanley.
