The 120-Day Charge-Off Delay: How Disputed Loans Protect ABS Warehouse Covenants

By Charles W. Kinslow IV, J.D., C.P.A. | Institutional Capital Markets & Accounting Analysis

Executive Summary: The Balance Sheet Incentive

Under SEC 10-K disclosures, Affirm does not recognize a defaulted loan as a formal accounting charge-off until 120 days past due. For institutional short-sellers and credit analysts, this lag represents a structural mechanism: keeping disputed balances in limbo prevents immediate delinquency spikes that could breach Asset-Backed Securities (ABS) warehouse facility covenants.

1. The Mechanics of Warehouse Credit Facilities

Affirm relies heavily on revolving warehouse credit facilities provided by global investment banks (such as Morgan Stanley, Barclays, and Goldman Sachs) to fund its point-of-sale originations. These facilities are collateralized by pools of consumer installment loans held in Variable Interest Entities (VIEs).

2. Performance Triggers & Delinquency Caps

Warehouse lending agreements contain strict financial maintenance covenants and performance triggers:

  • Maximum Delinquency Ratios: If 30+ day or 60+ day delinquencies in a collateral pool cross contractual thresholds (typically 2.5% to 3.5%), the warehouse lender can execute a cash-flow sweep or freeze borrowing capacity.
  • Cost of Capital Penalties: Exceeding risk bands widens interest spreads over SOFR, instantly eroding net interest margins.

3. The Short-Seller Thesis: "Dispute Parking"

When a consumer returns merchandise and disputes an unapplied refund, Affirm's systems classify the loan as "Under Active Merchant Investigation." Because the balance is categorized as a dispute rather than an acknowledged credit default, the platform delays recognizing the loss on its primary charge-off schedules for up to four full billing cycles (120 days).

Regulatory & Legal Oversight

The CFPB's landmark May 2024 Interpretive Rule applying Truth in Lending Act (TILA) / Regulation Z protections directly targets this practice. Lenders are legally required to investigate billing errors promptly and cannot report accounts as delinquent or assess finance charges during dispute pendency.
